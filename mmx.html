The Motivation Matrix

Motivation × Identity × Context × Time

The Motivation Matrix, MMX™, is a way of reading a situation before an answer is given. Four things are read together. Why the person is asking. Who they are in this moment. What is actually in front of them. What the clock is doing.

Why it matters

People do not arrive at a decision as averages. They arrive with a reason for asking, a sense of who they are, a situation around them, and a clock running.

The Motivation Matrix (MMX™) is a framework for reading incoming information through four factors — Motivation × Identity × Context × Time — before a response is formed. In the current framing, the stimulus is the information; MMX is the reading of it. Its output is a provisional interpretation the person can correct, not an account of hidden motives.

The four factors

Four factors, read together.

The spoken name is the Motivation Matrix. The four factors are Motivation, Identity, Context, and Time. The mark between them means each one changes how the others are read. It is not a score, and it is not a formula with weights.

Motivation

Stated goals, priorities, needs and willingness to act now.

Ask; do not infer an unconscious motive as fact.

Identity

Self-described roles, values, commitments, relevant experience.

Use roles the person states; no demographic profiling.

Context

Situational constraints, resources, relationships, available information.

Separate supplied text from missing facts.

Time

Sequence, changing circumstances, stated urgency, horizon.

Record a reported date as reported; never convert it into a verified deadline.
What a reading gives back

A provisional reading the person can correct.

Inputs: conversation, explicit self-report, user-selected documents and corrections, with the source of each input and any missing information noted. Task cues may inform a question, not establish a physiological state.

Output:

  1. what was supplied;
  2. a provisional interpretation;
  3. alternatives and unknowns;
  4. candidate framings or response approaches;
  5. one useful clarification request.
Uncertainty is stated qualitatively, with its reason; no numerical confidence is invented. When evidence is inadequate, MMX abstains, explains the gap and asks a bounded question.
Working with Ethics Pulse™

MMX interprets. Ethics Pulse reviews. The person chooses.

MMX interprets information. Ethics Pulse reviews candidate responses and process. The person chooses. This division of roles is a proposed way of explaining the system, not a description of separate software components. Neither component gets authority over the person.

Between the two, supplied facts, inferred readings, uncertainty, candidate framings, constraints and unresolved checks pass separately, so an assumption does not become a fact by crossing from one to the other.

How Ethics Pulse™ guides the response
Shaped to one person

A method, not a drop-in filter.

MMX™ is meant to be a method, not a drop-in filter. Each MMX™ companion is designed to be shaped to one person: their own account of their goals, priorities and working style, and the examples they choose to share, carried in that person's own standing instructions and living memory. Its working value is intended to come from how closely a companion fits one person's motivation, identity, context and time, which is why each one starts from that person rather than from a template.

The framework's central premise is that a person who is regulated is better placed to read what is in front of them and to choose. This is the framework's working premise, and MMX does not measure regulation.

What this is not. An early sketch explored a weighted product of the four factors. The weights were never set. MMX™ does not measure a nervous system.
Where the work stands

Scope and status.

Scope and statusMMX™ is a working framework in active use with many people. It is tested and refined through that use, with follow-up with users and positive feedback beginning to come in. It is not certified or independently validated.

Independent evaluation is a planned next step. Citations identify sources; the published research informs the design, and it does not by itself validate the framework.
