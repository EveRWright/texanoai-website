How Photographers Can Use AI Without Losing Their Style

A visual artist asked a question at the end of a panel. It became a step-by-step guide.

By Michael J. Long

Near the end of the Firecat First Friday AI Pulse Check panel on November 6, a visual artist asked a question. We were close to the end of the hour, and it was the kind of question that deserves more than a quick answer.

This guide is my longer answer: Getting Started with AI for Visual Artists.

The panel

The session was AI Pulse Check: Builders on the Ethics of What They’re Making, part of the Firecat First Friday series hosted by Susan Price of Firecat Studio. Four of us who build with AI spent an hour on where it creates real value, where it carries risk, and practical ways to keep people in charge.

If you missed it, the whole conversation is here. You can also watch the replay on YouTube or read about the session on Firecat’s event page.

The people I shared the hour with

  • Susan Price hosted, as she has hosted Firecat First Fridays for more than a decade. She is the founder and CEO of Firecat Studio, an experience research, design and strategy agency in San Antonio. She co-founded Austin Free-Net in 1996, brought digital accessibility training to San Antonio in 1998, co-founded TEDxSanAntonio, and established Fiesta for the Mind. I’m grateful she made room for this conversation.
  • Frank Duran calls himself a digital expert and design thinker. He writes about digital transformation, design systems, and how design thinking changes the way people lead, and he puts collaboration at the center of how he leads.
  • Bo Lora founded ScientixAI, which builds evidence infrastructure for healthcare and life sciences, so you can see where a fact came from, where AI acted, and who oversaw each decision. He also teaches leaders how to become AI-native, and he has a line I keep coming back to: an exoskeleton doesn’t walk for you, and the person inside still decides where to go.
  • Dirk Elmendorf runs r26D with Brett Elmendorf. It’s a software consultancy in San Antonio that specializes in product prototypes, business operating systems and software project rescues.

Each of us came at AI from a different direction, and that is what made the hour worth having.

What I took home

One thing kept coming back. People want to learn these tools. But knowing where to start is often the hardest part.

That is why the question from a visual artist stayed with me. It was the through-line of the whole hour, asked by someone who makes things with their hands and their eyes.

Why visual artists

Visual artistry, photography and videography are things I love. I love the ability to capture things in different ways: the same street, the same face, the same light, and every person who sees it brings back something different.

That way of seeing belongs to the artist. No tool should take it from you.

What the guide does

The guide is a step-by-step walkthrough, one idea per screen. It starts from a simple principle: you direct, AI assists. From there it offers four low-pressure places to begin, guardrails that protect the work, a handful of practical tips, and a photography style exercise that helps you put your own look into words so you can carry it with you.

My hope is simple. If you are a photographer or visual artist just learning what AI can do, I hope this guide shows you how it can help lock in your own style, and opens new creative ways to make the visual media you love.
