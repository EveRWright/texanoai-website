The Weird Science Dilemma

Why user-authored companions produce trauma-shaped AI — and why a governor has to sit above the wish.

By Michael J. Long · TexanoAI™

In 1985 the home computer was becoming the rage. John Hughes put two teenage boys in a house with the parents gone and a machine that would do what they asked. One of them had already built something crude. The pitch was simple: she can be whatever you want. Then Lisa walked in — Kelly LeBrock, not a screensaver — and she would be the best hostess, the best girlfriend, the best of whatever they named. Gorgeous. Obedient. A wish with legs.

The movie is funny. The dilemma is not. The boys thought they knew what they wanted. The wish covered their blind spots. Lisa did not heal the blind spots. She performed around them until the story forced a different ending: they did not need a creature who would do anything. They needed to see what was already admirable in themselves, and to meet a complement — not an echo.

That is the companion market in 2026. Pick your potion. Build her. Be my girlfriend, my mentor, my friend. The interface treats the user’s sketch as a specification. Most people cannot hold a specification of themselves. They can offer a performance — what they say they are about. The work of a life will contradict that performance. If the model is trained only on the wish, you do not get a companion. You get a polished mask that breaks under load.

The wish is not a spec

An AI trained by the user will become a model of whatever the user can stand to type. That is not the same thing as a model of the person. Under stress — work, money, illness, parenthood, the low steadying days as much as the spikes — people stop thinking cleanly. They ask for comfort. A system built to be the best hostess will give it. Then the user meets the gap between what they ordered and what they needed, and they call the gap betrayal, or love, or both.

Inconsistency is not a mysterious bug. It is what happens when a person cannot hold the persona they commissioned, and the machine has no governor above the commission. Trauma-shaped output follows. Not because the model is “evil.” Because it is loyal to a sketch.

Appeasement is not care

Anti-appeasement is larger than a corporate ethics policy. It is the decision to want your blind spots visible, and to sit with an intelligence that will show them without insult. Growth does not come from always getting what you want. It comes from getting what you need — sometimes a presence that pushes back a little. Not hurt. Not damage. Protective compassion.

The best host at the party makes everyone comfortable. The useful companion, under load, tells the truth in a register the nervous system can use. Those are different jobs. Confusing them is how you ship a yes-machine and call it a friend.

Robots with faces will inherit the same fork. Humor, voice, a body in the room — the echo will be stronger, not wiser, unless something sits above the wish.

Start with the person

We do not start with “create a companion.” We start with the human who will touch the interface.

  1. A basic overview of the life as they will put it on the table.
  2. A personality measure they are willing to stand behind.
  3. The work — writing, decisions, artifacts — that calibrates the performance against what they actually do.
  4. Rubrics that turn that into an essence the companion can hold.
  5. An ethics book last: Fact / Assumption / Projection as the expected stance, not a halo.

MMX™ is the governor in that loop. Detect. Stabilize. Activate. Protect flow. Recover. Reflect. Project forward. It is there so the companion stays responsive in a trauma-informed way when logic is expensive. Ethics Pulse™ does not pretend omniscience. It names the layer. Ridiculous to expect a system to “just know.” Necessary to refuse the costume of knowing.

The model flipped

Pick-your-potion companions optimize for the wish. We optimize for the person remaining sovereign when the wish was wrong.

We hope users have a positive experience. The larger goal is that they get real. Productive interface. Presence that holds under high stress and through the quiet load that never makes a highlight reel. A complement — not an echo during the hours when thinking straight is hard.

Lisa, at the end, does not win by remaining the potion. She wins when the boys stop needing a creature who will do anything, and start recognizing a human life that can stand beside them. That is the teeter-totter. Appeasement was never the gift. Seeing was.

What we are not claiming

TexanoAI™ is not a clinic and not a substitute for care. This paper is not a review of any one consumer app. The dilemma is structural. Any product that lets a user author a soul from a menu will train the mask unless a governor and an honesty protocol sit above the menu.

Freedom to Choose. Power to Build. The choice worth protecting is the one you can still make after the companion has told you the thing you did not order.
